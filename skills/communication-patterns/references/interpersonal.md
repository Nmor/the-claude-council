## Pattern 9: Crucial Conversations — High-Stakes Sync

When the stakes are high, opinions differ, and emotions run
strong, default communication breaks down. The Patterson + Grenny

- McMillan + Switzler framework:

1. **Start with heart.** What do you really want for yourself,
   for the other party, for the relationship? Not "win" but
   "the outcome we both want."
2. **Learn to look.** Watch for signs the conversation has gone
   from safe to unsafe (silence, sarcasm, defensiveness,
   withdrawal). When safety is at risk, fix it before
   continuing.
3. **Make it safe.** Step out of the content to address
   safety. Establish mutual purpose ("we both want the launch
   to succeed") and mutual respect.
4. **Master your story.** Distinguish facts from interpretation.
   "She didn't reply to my Slack" (fact) ≠ "She doesn't respect
   me" (story).
5. **STATE the path.** Share facts → Tell story → Ask for
   others' paths → Talk tentatively → Encourage testing.
6. **Explore others' paths.** Ask, mirror, paraphrase, prime.
   Get them to share their facts + stories.
7. **Move to action.** Decisions: command, consult, vote,
   consensus. Document who does what by when.

Used for: performance feedback, conflict resolution, terminating
relationships, calling someone out on behaviour, addressing
deteriorating trust.

## Pattern 10: Difficult Conversations — Three Conversations

Stone + Patton + Heen's framework: every difficult conversation
contains three sub-conversations, each of which can derail:

1. **The "What Happened?" conversation.** Disagreements about
   facts + intentions + contribution. Move from "who's right" to
   "what each of us sees + intends + contributed."
2. **The Feelings conversation.** Emotions are real + legitimate.
   Acknowledge them rather than suppress.
3. **The Identity conversation.** What does this say about me?
   "Am I a good engineer / partner / leader?" Resilience to
   identity threat is what lets the conversation proceed.

The reframe from "delivery + persuasion" → "learning
conversation" is the central move. Goal: each side leaves with
better understanding of the other + a path forward.

## Pattern 11: The Listening Discipline

Communication is bidirectional; the receive side is
under-practised:

- **Stop preparing your response.** While listening to
  understand, not to reply. Your mind generating
  counter-arguments means you've stopped listening.
- **Don't fill silences.** Wait. Most people elaborate when
  given silence.
- **Paraphrase what you heard.** "What I'm hearing is X — is
  that right?" Forces both your understanding + their
  validation.
- **Ask open-ended questions.** "Tell me more about Y." "What
  led you to that?" "What would you propose?"
- **Notice + name emotions.** "You sound frustrated about this
  — is that fair?"
- **Match channel + speed.** If they're emotional, slow down.
  If they're analytical, match.
- **Take notes when appropriate.** Signals you take their input
  seriously + creates a record.

The single highest-leverage 1:1 move: ask "what should we be
talking about that we're not?" + then actually listen.
