## Pattern 14: Written Excellence — Strunk + White Operational

Sentence-level discipline:

- **Omit needless words.** "In order to" → "to". "At this
  point in time" → "now". Every sentence loses 20-40% of its
  words in a good edit.
- **Active voice.** "The team shipped it" > "It was shipped by
  the team."
- **Concrete verbs.** "Decided" > "made a decision." "Failed"
  > "experienced challenges."
- **Parallel structure.** "Fast, reliable, and cheap" > "Fast,
  reliability, and cheaply."
- **Vary sentence length.** A short sentence after long ones
  hits hard. Like that.
- **End strong.** The last word of each sentence and paragraph
  carries emphasis.
- **Read it aloud.** Awkward sentences sound awkward.

Pinker's classic style: write to a reader who is alongside you
looking at the world. Avoid academic / corporate fog that
signals belonging to a tribe at the cost of communicating.

## Pattern 15: Edit Ruthlessly

First drafts are for thinking. Final drafts are for the reader.
The transformation requires:

- **Cut.** Most drafts shrink 30-50% in revision without losing
  meaning.
- **Reorder.** Move the answer to the top. Move context to where
  it's needed, not as preamble.
- **Replace.** Vague words → specific. Passive → active. Jargon
  → plain.
- **Sleep on it.** A 24-hour gap exposes problems you can't see
  while drafting.
- **Read aloud.** Catches awkwardness, rhythm problems, dead
  words.
- **Hand to a stranger.** They'll find the curse of
  knowledge spots.

The amateur drafts once and ships. The principal drafts, sleeps,
edits twice, hands to a peer, edits again, then ships.
