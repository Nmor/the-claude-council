## 7. Cliometrics + quantitative history

Cliometrics applies econometric and statistical methods to historical questions: demographic history
(parish registers), economic history (price series, trade volumes), prosopography (collective
biography of named groups). Foundational works: Robert Fogel + Stanley Engerman *Time on the Cross*
(1974, controversial), Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie *Montaillou* (1975) (microhistory + quantitative
parish records).

Pitfalls — and the Reinhart-Rogoff 2009 cautionary tale: published data series may contain errors
(the famous Excel spreadsheet error in *This Time Is Different*'s 90%-debt-to-GDP threshold, exposed
by Herndon, Ash + Pollin 2013, changed the apparent finding from "growth collapses above 90%" to
"growth modestly declines"). Always: cite data sources, publish data + code (FAIR principles per
research-methods skill), enable replication. Quantitative history without published data is faith.
