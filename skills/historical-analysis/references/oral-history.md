## 6. Oral history methodology

Oral history is the systematic recording of individuals' recollections — not casual interviews but
structured, archived, citable testimony. Per Ritchie + Thompson + Portelli:

**Pre-interview**: research the subject's biography, historical context, the events they witnessed.
Prepare open-ended questions (not yes/no) organised chronologically or thematically. Obtain informed
consent in writing (Oral History Association template) covering recording, transcription, deposit in
an archive, future use, restrictions.

**Interview**: minimise interviewer voice; let the subject speak. Use "tell me about..." not "did
you...". Note non-verbal cues, hesitations, emotional weight — these become part of the record.
Cover sequential events but be prepared to follow associative memory.

**Post-interview**: transcribe verbatim including pauses, false starts, laughter. Allow the subject
to review the transcript (some projects require this). Deposit in an archive with metadata (date,
location, equipment, consent terms). Cite as: Speaker, interview by Interviewer, location, date,
archive accession number.

Portelli's critical insight: oral history's "errors" (misdating, conflation, exaggeration) are
themselves historical evidence — evidence of how memory works, how meaning is made retrospectively,
what stories communities tell themselves. A factually wrong memory is not worthless data; it is data
about the speaker's interpretive frame.
