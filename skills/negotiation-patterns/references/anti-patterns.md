# negotiation-patterns: Anti-Patterns

> The fourteen recurring negotiation failures and the named correction for
> each. Pointed at by the SKILL.md row "Anti-Patterns".
>
> **Size budget: 8 KB** — `token-budget.mjs --check`.

## Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern 1: Positional Bargaining as Default

Trading positions ("I want $100, you want $50, let's meet at
$75") without exploring why each side wants what they want. The
$75 is rarely the value-maximising outcome — there are usually
multi-issue tradeoffs that produce $90 for both sides.

**Correction**: ask why. Ask about interests. Look for tradeoffs.
Reserve positional bargaining for the final phase of distributive
allocation, AFTER value creation is exhausted.

### Anti-Pattern 2: No BATNA Preparation

Walking into a negotiation without knowing your reservation value
or having a real alternative. Result: caving under pressure, or
walking away from deals that were actually better than your
alternative.

**Correction**: ALWAYS know your BATNA + reservation value before
the conversation. Write them down. Stick to them.

### Anti-Pattern 3: Win-at-All-Costs

Treating every negotiation as zero-sum + adversarial. Destroys
value creation, signals to the counterparty that they should also
defect, and produces fragile agreements the counterparty
underperforms on out of resentment.

**Correction**: distinguish single-shot transactions (where some
distributive intensity is fine) from repeated games (where joint
value creation matters more than per-deal capture).

### Anti-Pattern 4: Splitting the Difference Reflexively

When two anchors are on the table, defaulting to the midpoint.
This is anchoring working as designed — the side with the more
aggressive anchor wins.

**Correction**: refuse the midpoint anchor. Reframe (objective
criteria, different metric, package). Make a counter-anchor of
your own before any movement.

### Anti-Pattern 5: Premature Concessions

Conceding before the counterparty makes any movement, or conceding
without naming what you want in return ("OK, I'll drop the price"
without "in exchange for…"). Trains the counterparty that
concessions are free.

**Correction**: every concession is conditional + paired. "If you
can agree to X, I can move to Y." This signals you're not a soft
target + creates the tradeoff structure.

### Anti-Pattern 6: Confusing Position with Interest

Hearing "I need $150K base" and assuming the interest is the
$150K. Often the interest is "I need to support my mortgage" or "I
need to feel valued vs my prior offer." Multiple positions satisfy
each — sign-on bonus, equity, faster vesting, title.

**Correction**: ALWAYS probe for interest. "Why is that specific
number important?" "What problem is that solving?"

### Anti-Pattern 7: Failing to Walk Away

Sticking with a deal that's below your reservation value because
of sunk-cost (the meetings already invested) or fear (the deal
might collapse). The walkaway point exists precisely for the
moment you don't want to use it.

**Correction**: a strong BATNA + a pre-committed reservation value

- permission to walk. Walking away is sometimes the value-
maximising move.

### Anti-Pattern 8: Cultural Tone-Deafness

Bringing US-style fast, direct, confrontational bargaining to a
Japan, China, or Brazil counterparty. Or expecting a 6-month
relationship build before discussing terms with a New York
counterparty. Both produce ruptured deals.

**Correction**: research the counterparty's culture. Adapt pace,
formality, communication style, decision-cycle expectations.

### Anti-Pattern 9: Single-Issue Tunnel Vision

Negotiating only the headline number (price, salary, valuation)
when there are 10+ other negotiable issues that the counterparty
might value more or less than you. Result: you discover a deal
that gives them what they want at significant cost to you, when
trades were available.

**Correction**: prepare the full issue list BEFORE the meeting.
Score importance. Look for tradeoffs.

### Anti-Pattern 10: Treating Tactics as the Whole Game

Memorising Voss's mirroring + labels + calibrated questions and
applying them as a script. The counterparty notices. Tactics work
ONLY in service of genuine listening + genuine value creation.

**Correction**: tactics are the surface; preparation +
interest-discovery + tradeoff design are the substance. Use
tactics to surface information; use information to design value.

### Anti-Pattern 11: Lying

The temptation: misrepresent your BATNA ("I have another offer at
$150K" when you don't), misrepresent your authority, misrepresent
material facts. The professional cost when caught: catastrophic.
The personal cost: corrosion of judgement.

**Correction**: strategic silence + selective disclosure are
fine. Material lies are not. If you would be embarrassed to have
the conversation read aloud in court, don't say it.

### Anti-Pattern 12: Negotiating with the Wrong Person

Spending months negotiating terms with someone who lacks authority
to bind. Result: a "deal" that the actual decider rejects + a
re-negotiation from the new starting point.

**Correction**: early in the conversation: "Who else needs to be
involved for this to move forward? Do you have authority to bind?"

### Anti-Pattern 13: Ignoring Internal Negotiations

Treating internal cross-team conversations (resourcing, ownership
boundaries, roadmap priorities) as if they weren't negotiations.
Result: dominant teams get their way; subordinate teams accumulate
resentment that surfaces later as quit threats, knowledge silos,
or political resistance.

**Correction**: internal negotiations DESERVE the same preparation
as external. BATNA, interests, multi-issue tradeoffs, durable
agreements. Often more so — the relationship continues forever.

### Anti-Pattern 14: Conflating Empathy with Concession

Acknowledging the counterparty's perspective ("I see why this is
hard for you") and treating that as a substantive concession.
Empathy is free — it doesn't move the deal. Substantive
concessions are paid.

**Correction**: empathise generously (it's the cheap part);
concede precisely (it's the expensive part).
