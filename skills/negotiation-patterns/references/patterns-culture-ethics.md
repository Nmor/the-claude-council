## Pattern 11: Cross-Cultural Negotiation

Hofstede's dimensions + Brett 2014 + Meyer 2014 inform style:

| Dimension | High-end behaviour | Low-end behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power Distance | Hierarchy respected; defer to senior; decisions slow + escalated | Egalitarian; junior can challenge; decisions distributed |
| Individualism | Direct negotiation, fast decisions, individual reward | Group consensus required, longer cycles, relationship-first |
| Uncertainty Avoidance | Detailed contracts, explicit clauses, slow trust | Looser contracts, faster trust, more reliance on relationship |
| Communication style (Meyer) | Low-context: explicit, direct, "say what you mean" | High-context: implicit, indirect, much is inferred |
| Disagreement style (Meyer) | Confrontational: open disagreement is fine | Confrontation-avoiding: open disagreement is rude |

Examples (each is a tendency, not a rule):

- US: low-context, confrontational, individualist, fast-deal
- Germany: low-context, confrontational, structured + detailed
- Japan: high-context, confrontation-avoiding, group-decision,
  long relationship-first
- China: high-context, hierarchical, long-term, relationship-first
- Brazil: high-context, confrontation-avoiding (with humour),
  relationship-first
- France: low-context but disagreement is intellectual sport;
  principles-first persuasion

The principal-level move is to **research the counterparty's
culture** + **adapt pace, formality, and directness** while
keeping your interests intact.

## Pattern 12: Negotiation Ethics — What's Never Acceptable

Negotiation involves selective disclosure, strategic framing, and
tactical silence. It does NOT involve:

- **Misrepresenting material facts.** Lying about something the
  other side would reasonably rely on (your financial condition,
  the product's specifications, your authority to make the deal,
  your alternatives).
- **Bribery, threats of violence, blackmail.**
- **Bad-faith negotiation** — pretending to want a deal you have
  no intention of signing.
- **Misrepresenting authority to bind.** If you can't sign without
  your board, say so before the conversation begins.

The Shell (2018) bright-line test: **Would a reasonable juror
consider this fraud?** If yes, don't do it. Strategic ambiguity
about your bottom line is fine; lying about the price your
competitor offered is not.
