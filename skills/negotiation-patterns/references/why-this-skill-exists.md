## Why This Skill Exists

Negotiation appears in every senior-level role. Most people learn
it informally — by watching others, often badly. The cost of bad
negotiation:

- **Vendor deals** routinely leave 10-30% on the table by
  positional bargaining on price + ignoring tradeable issues.
- **Salary negotiations** cost engineers $50K-$200K over a career
  through under-asking + accepting first offers.
- **Internal resource allocations** turn into power games when
  framed as zero-sum, producing political residue that lasts
  years.
- **M&A + investor negotiations** are massively asymmetric: the
  other side has done thousands of these; first-time founders /
  acquirers learn at $50M per lesson.
- **Cross-cultural deals** collapse from style mismatch when the
  underlying interests were perfectly aligned.

Principal-level negotiation is a learnable craft. The frameworks
above — Harvard Method, BATNA discipline, multi-issue tradeoffs,
tactical empathy, contingency contracts, MESO — are taught at
business + law schools because they reliably produce better
outcomes.

This skill should fire when:

- A vendor contract is being negotiated (every clause is
  negotiable, including ones the vendor claims are "standard")
- A hiring offer is being constructed (multiple-issue trade with
  the candidate)
- A salary negotiation is happening (the employee deserves
  preparation discipline equal to the company's)
- Internal teams are arguing about ownership / priorities /
  resources
- An RFP / RFP-response is being prepared
- A board / investor conversation is being prepared
- A dispute is being resolved without litigation
- A partnership or JV is being structured

The principal-level response is to slow down, identify your BATNA

- reservation value, list the full issue set, probe the
counterparty's interests, look for trades, and reach durable
agreements both sides will perform on.

That's the entire purpose of this skill.

Standards cited grounding (verbatim, with editions):

- Fisher + Ury + Patton, *Getting to Yes* (3rd ed 2011) — the
  canonical interest-based framework.
- Ury, *Getting Past No* (1991/2007) — five-step breakthrough.
- Voss + Raz, *Never Split the Difference* (2016) — tactical
  empathy.
- Lax + Sebenius, *3-D Negotiation* (2006) — setup + deal design.
- Lax + Sebenius, *The Manager as Negotiator* (1986) — create /
  claim tension.
- Raiffa, *The Art and Science of Negotiation* (1982) — analytic
  foundation.
- Malhotra + Bazerman, *Negotiation Genius* (2007) — MESO,
  contingency contracts, post-settlement settlement.
- Shell, *Bargaining for Advantage* (3rd ed 2018) — ethics + style.
- Lewicki + Saunders + Barry, *Negotiation* (8th ed 2020) —
  comprehensive textbook.
- Hofstede + Hofstede + Minkov, *Cultures and Organizations* (3rd
  ed 2010) — cultural dimensions.
- Meyer, *The Culture Map* (2014) — eight scales.
- Brett, *Negotiating Globally* (3rd ed 2014) — cross-cultural
  joint gains.
- Mnookin + Peppet + Tulumello, *Beyond Winning* (2000) — with-
  lawyer negotiation.
- Galinsky + Schweitzer, *Friend & Foe* (2015) — cooperation +
  competition.

These are the principal-level references a negotiation practitioner
uses to ground tactics in evidence rather than intuition.
