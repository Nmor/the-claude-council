## Pattern 12: Citation + Reference Management

Every claim from a source gets a citation. Discipline:

- **Cite primary sources** (not blog summaries)
- **Use DOIs** (durable identifiers; bare URLs rot)
- **Manage references in a tool** (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote,
  Paperpile, BibTeX file)
- **Use a consistent citation style** (APA, MLA, Chicago,
  Vancouver, IEEE — pick one per artefact)
- **Match every in-text citation to a reference list entry +
  vice versa**
- **Include access dates for online sources** that may change
- **Note retraction notices** (retractionwatch.com)
- **Don't cite what you haven't read**

Re-reading the cited sources before final draft catches the
"telephone game" distortion that creeps in over many citation
generations.

## Pattern 13: Research Ethics

Research with human subjects requires:

- **Informed consent**: subjects know what the study is,
  what's expected, the risks, that they can withdraw
- **Voluntary participation**: no coercion (especially when
  researcher has power over subject — boss, professor, doctor)
- **Privacy + confidentiality**: data handled per GDPR, HIPAA,
  CCPA + the IRB's approved protocol
- **Beneficence**: the study's expected benefit (knowledge)
  outweighs the risk
- **Justice**: subjects + populations who bear the risk also
  benefit from the knowledge
- **IRB / ethics review**: for any research with human
  subjects in academic / medical context; increasingly
  expected in industry research too

For corporate research (UX, A/B testing, market research):
parallel principles apply. Don't experiment on users in ways
that affect their welfare without consent. The Facebook
emotional-contagion study (2014) is the canonical "what not to
do" example.

For data research: respect data licenses, attribution
requirements, privacy regulations.

## Pattern 14: Triangulation

When stakes are high, no single source or method is enough.
Triangulate:

- **Methodological triangulation**: quantitative + qualitative
  on the same question
- **Source triangulation**: multiple independent sources of the
  same claim
- **Investigator triangulation**: multiple researchers reach the
  same conclusion independently
- **Theoretical triangulation**: same phenomenon viewed
  through multiple theoretical lenses

When triangulation agrees, confidence rises. When it disagrees,
the disagreement itself is data — investigate why.
